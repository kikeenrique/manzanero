# Feature status

For one table of what works on which platform, read [status at a glance](status.md) first. This page is the compact inventory of what is available now and how far each area has been checked. “Verified” always means a scoped live result on the recorded device and environment; it is not a general reliability guarantee. [Verification](verification.md) holds the evidence and the failures, the [roadmap](roadmap.md) orders the remaining work, and the [live check plan](live-check-plan.md) lists the hardware runs still owed.

Unless a row says otherwise, physical evidence comes from one iPhone 13 mini (iOS 27.2.1); the Apple TV 4K, the iPad mini and the Apple Watch are named where they ran, and simulator evidence from iOS 27.2 runtime simulators.

The tool runs on an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 26.6 or later and Xcode 27 or later. Betas are published as prereleases on [GitHub](https://github.com/kikeenrique/manzanero/releases) and install with `brew install kikeenrique/tap/manzanero`; they have been installed on the development Mac and, since 2026-11-23, on a second Mac.

## Unavailable or missing

- Simulator `wake`: refused as not implemented (`unsupported-operation`). Simulator `paste` is refused: `devicectl` reaches a simulator's clipboard since 2026-11-21, but the Command+V after it inserted nothing ([simulator input](simulator-input.md#unicode-text-set-value)). `type` of text the named layout cannot produce is refused in favour of `set-value`, on both platforms.
- Physical `logs` ([app logs](app-logs.md#physical-devices)): a stream from now, at most 600 seconds, of one process until it ends, a group or every process; the device's stored log is not read. It is the only way to debug-level lines from a phone: `console` mirrors info and above. Built on 2026-11-20 and checked on the iPhone 13 mini the same evening, and on the Apple TV 4K's paired tunnel on 2026-11-21. On an Apple Watch (2026-11-25) the relay sent nothing.
- `hid-info`, live raw `ax-snapshot`, independent Wi-Fi pairing (`pair wifi`) and `--pairing-file` are no longer commands. Pair through Xcode/CoreDevice; offline `decode-ax-archive` still works.
- One tree for a browser and its page: with a web page in front the tree is the page alone. The browser's own controls are reached by giving the browser's PID to a selector action, which ran on the phone on 2026-11-21 (Safari's address bar) ([web content](direct-accessibility.md#web-content)). Reading, `wait`, `press` and typing work on a page in Chrome and, since 2026-11-21, in Safari on the one phone, and since 2026-11-22 in an app's own web view inset from the screen's corner, where `scroll` was verified too (phone and simulator), and on a page with frames (phone); `set-value` does not change a web input in either. On a simulator Safari is in front and the page is a process it shows: since 2026-11-21 `press`, `scroll`, `set-value` and `wait` take the page's PID there (`--pid`, `WebViewProcessID` in Safari's tree), and a press by label worked on a test page; taps and `type` always did.
- Elements with neither label nor identifier, such as the Settings search field, are selected by their current value with `--with-value`, run on a simulator and, on 2026-11-22, on the phone's Settings search field; an element with no value either cannot be selected. One of several elements that share a label, which is common on web pages, is chosen with `--index` ([guide](direct-accessibility.md#current-guarded-native-press)).
- [Device settings](device-settings.md) (`rotate`, `settings`, `location`, `status-bar`, `clipboard`, added on 2026-11-21 through devicectl) cover appearance, text size, contrast and reduced motion, and since 2026-11-23 VoiceOver and StandBy, and since 2026-11-24 biometric enrolment (each run on a simulator; VoiceOver on and off on the phone too, where its first switch-on raised an explanation and a notification permission prompt); `audio` and `reset` are not exposed. `status-bar` is refused on a device, which does not support it. The rest ran on an iPhone simulator and on the phone.
- System gestures (Home, the Centres, back) are not presets: on a simulator such touch sequences reach the app, not the system; on the phone a swipe down from a top corner opens Control Centre or Notification Centre, and one up from the bottom edge stays with the app, so Home is `button home` ([presets](gesture-sequences.md#presets)). `double-tap` is one, added on 2026-11-21 and run on a simulator and the phone.
- A permission prompt has no command of its own, by choice: its buttons carry labels and no identifiers, and `press --label '^Allow' --match regex` answers it ([recipe](direct-accessibility.md#current-native-tree-route)).

Those retired routes used to reach AXe, idevice or ipb helpers. The CLI no longer contains them (see [retired helpers](dependencies.md#retired-helpers)), and the execution policy would refuse those binaries anyway.

User-facing live mirroring, noticing a permission or Face ID prompt without being told to watch for it, a press at a point of the first-generation Siri Remote's touch surface (the second generation's clickpad ring is the arrows, already sent), device-side release after an uncertain one and a complete assertion system are missing or incomplete.

## How to read progress

Use three separate questions: is there an executable route, has that route been checked on this hardware, and does it recover from interruption/failure? A feature can answer yes to the first two and still be unsuitable for unattended use. No percentage is assigned, because counting commands would hide differences in scope and evidence.
